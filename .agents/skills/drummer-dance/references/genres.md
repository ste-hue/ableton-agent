# Dance drum genre priorities

These are decision priors, not patterns. Combine them with the user's adjectives,
the current Set, and an original rhythmic claim. Tempo ranges are starting points.

## House

- Usually 120-126 BPM; continuity, offbeat lift, disco/funk-derived motion.
- Stable four-on-floor foundation; human motion lives in hats, shakers, claps,
  percussion, velocity, and small timing disagreements.
- Prefer gradual additions/removals and long send/filter arcs over abrupt edits.
- Open hats and claps should breathe; avoid filling every sixteenth.

## Tech house

- Usually 124-130 BPM; dry low end, strong negative space, compact syncopation.
- Kick is authoritative; bass and low percussion answer it rather than blur it.
- Short claps, rims, shuffled hats, and one memorable counter-rhythm carry identity.
- Use surgical 8-bar omissions, restrained fills, quick room sends, and decay
  automation that tightens before drops or opens into breaks.

## Techno

- Usually 128-145 BPM; hypnosis, timbral development, subtraction, machine intent.
- Repetition earns its place through slow changes in tone, decay, drive, and space.
- Layer kicks only with clear frequency/transient roles and phase-conscious tuning.
- Macro drama comes from removing the floor, changing spectral weight, or revealing
  a new machine voice—not from constant snare fills.

## Leftfield techno

- Usually 125-145 BPM; asymmetric cycles, unusual timbres, displaced expectations.
- Keep one stable reference while another layer works in a 3-, 5-, or 7-hit cycle.
- Use found/metallic/percussive color, but preserve functional frequency slots.
- Automation may be less symmetrical, yet important arrivals still land on the
  composition's chosen phrase boundaries.

## French house

- Usually 118-128 BPM; swung disco energy, compression as motion, filtered reveals.
- Drums converse with chopped or filtered musical material; leave room for pumping.
- Crisp kick/clap core, bright hats, tambourine/shaker lift, occasional tom fills.
- Use long filter and send arcs, parallel color, and section-scale saturation; do
  not flatten the drum transients or imitate a specific record.

## Indie dance

- Usually 105-125 BPM; hybrid live/machine character, toms, claps, roomy gestures.
- Allow controlled imperfection and wider dynamics than strict club-tool techno.
- Use fills as performed phrases, not generic rolls; contrast dry verses/sections
  with wider choruses or instrumental arrivals.
- Saturation can be grainier and effects more audible, while the kick remains usable.

## Adjacent modern dance styles

When the named genre is not listed, place it on these axes before writing:

- straight ↔ swung;
- sparse ↔ dense;
- dry ↔ spacious;
- stable pulse ↔ displaced pulse;
- clean transient ↔ saturated body;
- gradual evolution ↔ hard edits.

State the chosen position briefly, then build from it rather than forcing the style
into the nearest listed template.
